================================================================================
MENTAL HEALTH EXPERT LLM — DATA SOURCES REFERENCE
================================================================================
Last updated: 2026-04-15

This document records the chosen data sources for both vector databases,
the justification for each inclusion and exclusion, and the exact document
schema each scraper must produce.

================================================================================
DB 1 — CLINICAL KNOWLEDGE CORPUS
================================================================================

PURPOSE
-------
Factual, psychoeducational content. Used when the user asks what a condition
is, what causes it, what the symptoms are, or how it is typically treated.
Responses grounded in this DB should read like a knowledgeable, plain-language
explanation from a health professional — not a clinical note and not a forum post.

SOURCES INCLUDED
----------------

1. NHS — National Health Service (nhs.uk/mental-health)
   License  : Open Government Licence v3.0 (free to reuse, adapt, redistribute)
   Content  : Condition overviews, symptoms, causes, diagnosis, treatment,
              self-help, and living-with sections. Written for the general public
              in plain UK English. Well-structured, consistently formatted,
              and updated regularly by NHS editorial staff.
   Why      : Gold-standard public-health source for UK audiences. Covers ~25
              mental health conditions with multi-section depth. OGL license
              removes all legal ambiguity. The section-per-page URL structure
              makes targeted extraction straightforward.

2. NICE — National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (nice.org.uk)
   License  : Open Government Licence v3.0
   Content  : "Information for the public" leaflets derived from clinical
              guidelines. Each leaflet summarises the official NICE guideline
              for a condition in plain language — covering what the condition is,
              how it is diagnosed, and what the recommended treatments are.
   Why      : NICE guidelines are the definitive UK clinical standard. The
              public-facing versions are accessible without clinical jargon.
              Complements NHS content by adding guideline-backed treatment
              recommendations that NHS condition pages sometimes omit.

3. NIMH — National Institute of Mental Health (nimh.nih.gov)
   License  : US Federal Government — public domain (no copyright restrictions)
   Content  : Health topic pages and brochures covering conditions, statistics,
              symptoms, risk factors, and treatment. US-oriented but globally
              accurate for clinical content.
   Why      : Highest-authority US source. Public domain removes any scraping
              concern. Adds prevalence statistics and research-backed framing
              that enrich the clinical corpus. Covers several conditions (e.g.
              specific anxiety subtypes) where NHS coverage is thinner.

4. WHO mhGAP — Mental Health Gap Action Programme (who.int)
   License  : Creative Commons Attribution-NonCommercial-ShareAlike 3.0
   Content  : mhGAP Intervention Guide web pages covering depression, psychosis,
              bipolar, epilepsy, substance use, dementia, and other priority
              conditions. Global framing, concise condition summaries, and
              emphasis on recognition and community-level support.
   Why      : International authority adds cross-cultural legitimacy. The mhGAP
              framing (designed for non-specialist health workers) is closer to
              the register we want than DSM-style clinical prose. CC license
              is compatible with our use.

5. Mind UK (mind.org.uk)
   License  : Content shared for personal, non-commercial use — standard web
              terms. Scraping for model training is not explicitly permitted but
              is a grey area for non-commercial research. Verify before
              commercial deployment.
   Content  : A-Z of mental health problems with sections on what it feels like,
              symptoms, causes, treatment, and self-care. Plain, empathetic
              language. Includes lived-experience framing that other sources lack.
   Why      : Mind's writing register is closest to how a mental health support
              tool should speak. It bridges clinical accuracy and human warmth.
              The "what it feels like" sections are uniquely valuable — they
              describe subjective experience, not just diagnostic criteria.

6. Centre for Clinical Interventions — CCI (cci.health.wa.gov.au)
   License  : Free for personal, educational, and clinical use. Explicitly
              states resources may be reproduced for non-commercial purposes.
   Content  : Psychoeducation modules (web + PDF) for anxiety, depression, OCD,
              eating disorders, and more. Each module includes a "what is it"
              section, a section on the maintenance cycle, and a section on
              how therapy helps. Written by clinical psychologists.
   Why      : CCI's psychoeducation modules are the standard material used in
              NHS IAPT and psychology training. They explain conditions in terms
              of mechanisms (e.g., the vicious cycle of avoidance) rather than
              just symptom lists — extremely valuable for generating responses
              that explain *why* something happens, not just *what* it is.

SOURCES EXCLUDED — DB 1
-----------------------

Mayo Clinic (mayoclinic.org)
   Reason   : Terms of Service explicitly prohibit automated scraping and
              systematic downloading of content. NHS + NIMH + NICE cover the
              same clinical ground with cleaner licensing. Risk of legal
              exposure is not justified by marginal content gain.

DSM-5 / ICD-11 diagnostic criteria (verbatim)
   Reason   : DSM-5 is copyright American Psychiatric Association. ICD-11 is
              WHO copyright (not the same license as mhGAP materials). More
              importantly, verbatim diagnostic criteria would push the model
              toward diagnostic language — the tool should explain conditions,
              not issue diagnoses.

Academic research papers (PubMed, etc.)
   Reason   : Register mismatch. Research abstracts are written for clinicians
              and researchers, not for people in distress. Retrieval would
              surface highly technical language at the wrong moment. Evidence
              is already baked into NHS/NICE/NIMH content.

WebMD / Healthline / Verywell Mind (health content farms)
   Reason   : Quality is inconsistent, editorial standards are lower than NHS
              or NIMH, and content is often SEO-optimised rather than
              clinically rigorous. Also frequently contain ads and affiliate
              links embedded in content.

Reddit / forums for clinical content
   Reason   : Not a clinical source. Peer accounts of symptoms and experiences
              are valuable for the dialogue fine-tuning corpus but must not
              enter the factual clinical DB — the risk of medical misinformation
              entering retrieval is too high.

Medication information (any source)
   Reason   : Out of scope for a conversational mental health support tool.
              Prescribing advice requires a licensed clinician. Including it
              creates liability and the risk of harm.

================================================================================
DB 2 — THERAPY TECHNIQUES CORPUS
================================================================================

PURPOSE
-------
Actionable, intervention-style content. Used when the user asks how to cope,
what to try, or how to practise a specific technique. Responses grounded in
this DB should read like a therapist explaining and demonstrating a tool —
clear steps, concrete examples, and an explanation of why it helps.

SOURCES INCLUDED
----------------

1. Centre for Clinical Interventions — CCI Therapy Workbooks (cci.health.wa.gov.au)
   License  : Free for non-commercial use.
   Content  : Full CBT and DBT workbooks in PDF and web format. Covers cognitive
              restructuring, behavioural activation, exposure hierarchies,
              thought records, worry management, problem solving, and more.
              Each module has psychoeducation + worked examples + blank worksheets.
   Why      : The most clinically rigorous free source available. These workbooks
              are used by clinical psychologists and NHS IAPT practitioners. The
              worked examples (showing a filled-in thought record, for instance)
              are exactly the kind of content that makes retrieved chunks
              immediately actionable.

2. Therapist Aid (therapistaid.com)
   License  : Free for clinical and educational use (per their terms).
   Content  : 200+ technique worksheets and educational articles. Covers CBT,
              DBT, ACT, mindfulness, motivational interviewing, grief, trauma,
              and more. Each worksheet has a description, purpose, and
              instructions. Articles provide step-by-step explanations.
   Why      : The most comprehensive free worksheet library available. The
              description text on each worksheet page concisely captures the
              technique name, purpose, target problem, and instructions — maps
              almost perfectly to our schema. High practitioner adoption means
              the content has been field-tested.

3. GetSelfHelp (getselfhelp.co.uk)
   License  : Free for personal and clinical use (Carol Kennerley / BABCP).
   Content  : CBT self-help resources — thought records, cognitive distortion
              explanations, behavioural activation sheets, relaxation scripts,
              grounding techniques, and problem-solving worksheets.
   Why      : Written by an accredited CBT therapist. Particularly strong on
              explaining cognitive distortions with examples and the mechanics
              of CBT homework. The plain-language style is ideal for retrieval
              into a conversational context.

4. Anxiety Canada (anxietycanada.com)
   License  : Free to use (non-commercial educational use).
   Content  : Anxiety-specific technique guides — SPACE, facing fears step by
              step, relaxation scripts, worry exposure, problem solving for
              anxiety, and sleep tools. Includes child/adult variants.
   Why      : Specialised focus on anxiety makes this a strong complement to
              general CBT sources. Their "how to" guides are unusually detailed
              — they include example dialogues and troubleshooting tips that
              most other sources omit.

5. ACT Mindfully (actmindfully.com.au)
   License  : Free resources section — explicitly available for download and use.
   Content  : ACT metaphors, defusion exercises, values worksheets, committed
              action guides, psychological flexibility explanations. Written by
              Russ Harris (the primary ACT author/trainer).
   Why      : Russ Harris is the leading ACT practitioner author. His materials
              are the standard reference for ACT implementation. The free
              resources include some of the clearest explanations of defusion
              and acceptance techniques available anywhere.

6. Self-Compassion (self-compassion.org)
   License  : Exercises available for free use (Kristin Neff's site).
   Content  : Guided self-compassion exercises — self-compassion break,
              compassionate letter writing, common humanity meditation,
              loving-kindness (Metta) practice, self-compassion journal prompts.
   Why      : Kristin Neff is the originator of MSC (Mindful Self-Compassion)
              and the primary research author in this area. Her exercises are
              the canonical reference. Self-compassion techniques are
              particularly important for the model — many users will present
              with self-critical language and shame, and standard CBT techniques
              don't address this as directly.

7. Positive Psychology (positivepsychology.com)
   License  : Free articles (standard web terms — grey area for training).
   Content  : Targeted articles on specific PPT techniques — three good things,
              best possible self, character strengths, savoring, gratitude
              interventions, and flourishing exercises. Each article includes
              step-by-step instructions and the research basis.
   Why      : Positive psychology tools are underrepresented in clinical
              worksheet libraries but have strong evidence for depression and
              wellbeing maintenance. Scraping specific high-quality articles
              (rather than bulk-crawling the site) minimises licensing risk.

8. DBT Self Help (dbtselfhelp.com)
   License  : Public resource site, free to use.
   Content  : Detailed breakdowns of all four DBT skill modules — mindfulness,
              distress tolerance, emotion regulation, and interpersonal
              effectiveness. Each skill has its own page with definition,
              rationale, and instructions.
   Why      : The most comprehensive free DBT skills reference structured by
              individual skill rather than by module overview. The page-per-skill
              structure maps cleanly to our document schema — one technique
              per chunk.

9. IAPT Self-Help Guides (NHS IAPT programme)
   License  : Open Government Licence (NHS-produced materials).
   Content  : Structured self-help workbooks from the NHS Improving Access to
              Psychological Therapies programme. PDFs covering low-intensity
              CBT interventions — behavioural activation, worry management,
              sleep hygiene, graded exposure, and activity scheduling.
   Why      : IAPT materials are the standard low-intensity CBT intervention
              used in primary care in England. They are highly structured,
              clinician-validated, and designed specifically for self-directed
              use — exactly the use case we're targeting.

SOURCES EXCLUDED — DB 2
-----------------------

Psychology Tools (psychologytools.com) — paid tier
   Reason   : The majority of their worksheet PDFs are behind a subscription
              paywall ($149/year for practitioners). Their free articles are
              included via direct URL targeting in therapist_aid-style scraping,
              but the paid PDFs are excluded. The free content from CCI and
              Therapist Aid covers the same techniques.

Mindfulness apps (Headspace, Calm) content
   Reason   : Proprietary content behind paywalls. Scraped content would violate
              ToS and copyright.

Therapist blogs (general)
   Reason   : Originally considered but excluded for quality-control reasons.
              Therapist blogs vary enormously in accuracy, may reflect personal
              opinion rather than evidence-based practice, and are harder to
              attribute. The curated sources above provide better coverage with
              known provenance.

Reddit (r/mentalhealth, r/anxiety, r/depression)
   Reason   : Moved to fine-tuning data pipeline — not a RAG source. See
              architecture decision notes in architecture.drawio. Dialogue
              style from Reddit is used to shape the model's tone via SFT/DPO
              training, not to supply technique content at inference time.

YouTube transcripts (therapy technique videos)
   Reason   : Transcript quality is highly variable, speaker attribution is
              unclear, and licensing is complex. The same techniques are
              covered more cleanly by the written sources above.

DOCUMENT SCHEMAS
================================================================================

DB 1 — CLINICAL KNOWLEDGE — Record Schema
------------------------------------------
Each record represents one section of one condition from one source.
Chunking at the section level (not the full condition page) gives retrieval
the right granularity — a query about "causes of anxiety" should not also
retrieve "treatment for anxiety" in the same chunk.

{
  "condition"    : str,        // Canonical condition name
                               // e.g. "Generalised Anxiety Disorder"
  "aliases"      : [str],      // Alternate names and abbreviations
                               // e.g. ["GAD", "generalised anxiety"]
  "source"       : str,        // One of: NHS | NICE | NIMH | WHO | Mind UK | CCI
  "source_url"   : str,        // Exact URL the content was scraped from
  "section"      : str,        // One of: overview | symptoms | causes |
                               //   risk_factors | diagnosis | treatment |
                               //   self_help | when_to_seek_help | living_with
  "content"      : str,        // The cleaned plain-text content of this section
  "icd11_code"   : str | null, // ICD-11 code for grounding (e.g. "6A70")
  "last_scraped" : str         // ISO date string (YYYY-MM-DD)
}

DB 2 — THERAPY TECHNIQUES — Record Schema
------------------------------------------
Each record represents one technique from one source. Some fields (steps,
worked_example, target_conditions) will be extracted by the pipeline from
raw_content using an LLM — they are left as null in raw data files and
populated at ingestion time.

{
  "technique_name"    : str,        // Canonical technique name
                                    // e.g. "Cognitive Restructuring"
  "aliases"           : [str],      // e.g. ["thought challenging", "thought records"]
  "modality"          : str,        // CBT | DBT | ACT | MBSR | MSC | PPT
                                    //   | relaxation | behavioral | interpersonal
  "target_conditions" : [str],      // Conditions this technique addresses
                                    // e.g. ["depression", "anxiety"]
  "target_problem"    : str,        // Specific problem addressed
                                    // e.g. "rumination" | "avoidance" | "emotional dysregulation"
  "steps"             : str,        // Step-by-step instructions (numbered)
  "worked_example"    : str | null, // Concrete example or sample dialogue
  "when_to_use"       : str,        // Context / appropriate moment for the technique
  "estimated_time"    : str,        // e.g. "5-10 minutes" | "ongoing"
  "difficulty"        : str,        // beginner | intermediate | advanced
  "raw_content"       : str,        // Full scraped text (pipeline extracts above fields)
  "source"            : str,        // Source name
  "source_url"        : str,        // Exact URL
  "last_scraped"      : str         // ISO date string
}

NOTE ON PIPELINE ENRICHMENT
----------------------------
For DB2, the structured fields (target_conditions, target_problem, steps,
worked_example, when_to_use, estimated_time, difficulty) are not reliably
extractable by CSS selectors alone — each source formats this differently.
These fields will be populated during the vector DB ingestion pipeline using
a structured LLM extraction pass over raw_content. The schema above defines
what the *final* ingestion record looks like, not what the raw scraper saves.

Raw scraper output for DB2 will always contain at minimum:
  technique_name, modality, raw_content, source, source_url, last_scraped
